# Discipline Module

Use this module for a field of knowledge, theory, methodology, research area, professional practice, or learning domain. Build conceptual orientation before evaluating claims or recommending a learning path.

## Discover

- Define the focal question, vocabulary, scope, and neighboring disciplines.
- Identify foundational concepts, major methods, canonical evidence, active debates, and practical applications.
- Separate broadly accepted knowledge, contested interpretations, historical context, and frontier questions.
- Prefer primary sources, standards, textbooks, or authoritative surveys when precision matters.

## Map

Use the relevant dimensions:

- central questions and object of study;
- concepts, definitions, and their dependencies;
- methods of inquiry or practice and what counts as evidence;
- schools, models, disagreements, and limitations;
- applications, prerequisites, and a sensible route from basics to competent use.

## Assess

Assess the discipline against the user's intended use: literacy, study, research, decision-making, or practice. Do not present a contested model as settled fact or equate popularity with evidential strength.
